The bar's drinks. The loser of every hand drinks the stake: one, doubled for each rung of a duel. Each seat has a tolerance, and the six drinks up to it are the danger zone: the seat shows in danger from the first of them. Which of those six is the last is drawn once at the deal, evenly, and kept secret, so in the zone each further drink is a 1 in 6 chance of passing out, then 1 in 5, 1 in 4, 1 in 3, 1 in 2, and the sixth is certain. Russian roulette without re-spinning: the cylinder is set once. Nothing else is random about it, and nobody sees the cap or the drawn drink, your own seat included; the flag says at risk, never next one. Calling a count exact sobers you one. The ship's dice are lives in the open: lose a hand, lose a die, no draw.

    
This is the host-sim table: the same match loop every difficulty number was measured on, with you at seat 0. When a bid is challenged the two of them can double the stake back and forth (counter, escalate) before the cups come up, as deep as nerve allows with a hard stop at 32x; the loser pays the stake that stood, in full. Those rungs are yours when you are in the duel: stand, escalate, or (as the challenger) fold for the reduced penalty. So is a chain-kill sweep (challenge a run of recent bidders at once, at a depth you pick; each swept seat answers on its own bid, and you answer them), and the pause after a rival's bid, where you can call the count exact (a hit heals a die or sobers a drink, a miss costs one) or, with the out-of-turn rule on, challenge it now. Every decision that costs you is yours. In the bar every bid is 飞 (ones are wild) or 斋 (ones count only as ones): a bid of ones is always 斋, any other face can be bid either way, and switching mid-round is priced. On the ship ones are wild, and a bid of aces counts aces alone and cannot open.
